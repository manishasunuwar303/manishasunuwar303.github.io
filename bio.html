My journey into business analysis began in Kathmandu, where a degree in Information Technology taught me to see organisations as systems — databases, processes, and the people who depend on them. I was less interested in how technology works than in how it creates value.

That curiosity led me to Cloud Factory, where I spent three years as a Data Specialist supporting high-growth retail-technology clients like Ibotta and Pathr.ai. Working inside Agile teams, I learned the craft that still defines me: turning ambiguous client needs into precise, prioritised requirements — and holding a standard of quality, 98% accuracy, even in the most complex workflows.

Alongside that, I explored the other side of business at Falcon Tech, a digital marketing agency, where I created content and presented on camera. It taught me something requirements documents can't: how people actually make decisions, and how to communicate an idea clearly enough that a stranger will act on it.

Wanting to connect technology with strategy, I moved to the UK for an MSc in International Business Management at Wrexham University. My dissertation — later reworked into a stakeholder-ready business case — grounded marketing-investment decisions in real qualitative research, and confirmed what I value most: turning evidence into decisions people can act on.

Today I bring all of that together. At Primark, I analyse sales and inventory data to reshape operational processes — reducing overstock by over 90% and helping my department reach a Top 5 national ranking. As a volunteer language mentor, I help young learners find their own relevance in what they study. Across every role, one thread holds: I listen closely, map what's really happening, and design the clearest path to a better outcome.

I'm a Business Analyst because I believe the most valuable thing you can do for a business is to understand it — genuinely — and then help it change.
